Technical background — what the stuck part is, and why access is hard

This page summarizes how a trip-lever bath waste works and what the trade literature says about removing or replacing it when it seizes — with a source link for every claim. It is context for the option analysis, not a how-to for this job.

1 · Anatomy: trip lever = part of a “waste & overflow” assembly

ComponentWhat it doesSource
Overflow plate + trip lever The faceplate at tub level with the lever; the lever raises/lowers a plunger inside the overflow pipe. Behind the plate is a linkage connected to a plunger: lever up = plunger up = tub drains; lever down = plunger seats = tub holds water. This Old House — How To Replace a Tub Drain
Waste & overflow assembly The pipe assembly under/behind the tub that connects the drain (shoe/strainer) and the overflow to the trap. The trade name for the whole unit is a Waste & Overflow Assembly. DIY Stack Exchange — trade terminology · Watco installation sheet (manufacturer PDF)
Linkage + plunger Adjustable brass linkage with a cylindrical plunger that slides inside the overflow pipe; adjustment sets tub water depth. Extracted through the overflow opening after removing two plate screws — when it is not seized. This Old House · Watco PDF

2 · Why the lever can be impossible to remove from above

Cause documented in trade/DIY literatureConsequence for removalSource
Decades of corrosion can fuse the plunger/bell to the pipe (a documented case: “the little bell on the inside was completely fused with the pipe and probably had been for a couple decades”). Linkage will not pull out; the practical fix reported was replacing the whole waste & overflow — which in that case was done by going “thru the drywall.” r/Plumbing thread (documented case)
Damage risk: forcing the removal can destroy the pipe assembly under/behind the tub; the standard warning is that a damaged assembly means “opening up the wall behind or beside the tub” or access from below. This matches the owner-reported position of the 5+ plumbers who declined the from-above attempt. DIY Stack Exchange · HomeOwnersHub thread
Even the plate screws themselves can be corroded solid; trade-forum guidance covers cutting/acid techniques — and confirms full replacement requires access from behind or below. Supports sequencing: non-invasive attempts first, structural access only as a deliberate step. HomeOwnersHub thread

Forum/DIY threads are practitioner reports, not official standards; they are cited only as documented case history, clearly separated from manufacturer and publisher guidance above.

3 · The three options in trade terms

OptionWhat trade literature saysAccess neededSources
A. Extract from above Standard first step: remove the two overflow-plate screws and “carefully pull out the brass waste-and-overflow linkage”; adjust or replace the linkage. Works when the mechanism moves freely. None — through the overflow opening This Old House
B. Replace assembly from below/behind Full replacement of a waste & overflow requires access to the underside (“From below, align the rubber gasket and drain elbow with the shoe”); trade literature states a damaged assembly “will be looking at opening up the wall behind or beside the tub” or gaining access from below. Ceiling opening below, or wall opening behind/beside the tub This Old House · DIY Stack Exchange
C. Convert the stopper type Convert a trip-lever tub to a lift-and-turn (or push-pull) stopper with a blank overflow plate; “unless there's an internal obstruction preventing the removal of the innards of the existing trip lever drain, there shouldn't be a need to either remove the tub or gain access behind the wall.” None — if the seized internals can be pulled Family Handyman — conversion guide · r/Plumbing (practitioner note)

Note on from-above retrofit products: a search was made for top-install waste & overflow retrofits (e.g., WingTite). The verified manufacturer page shows WingTite is a top-install drain for fiberglass/plastic shower stalls, not for bathtub waste & overflow — it is not applicable here (WingTite official page). No manufacturer source was found for a from-above full waste & overflow retrofit; if a provider claims one, ask for the exact product and its manufacturer instructions.
