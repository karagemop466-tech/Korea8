Executive summary

Compiled 2026-11-07 from sources retrieved and read on the same day

The trip lever cannot be freed from the tub side by any of the five-plus plumbers already consulted, so the job now turns on two decisions: whether a licensed firm can be found that will open the ceiling below and restore it under one contract, and how the permit question is settled in writing before work starts.

The situation

  • A bathtub waste-and-overflow trip lever is stuck in the concealed plumbing behind the wall, in a tenant-occupied San Francisco flat. The overflow is in a hard-to-reach position and opening drywall may be needed to reach the assembly.
  • Five or more licensed plumbers have already declined to remove it from above, citing the risk of destroying the concealed plumbing; they recommend working from below.
  • Below the tub is a storage unit, so access from below likely means opening and restoring its drywall ceiling. That makes the job a two-trade contract (plumbing plus drywall) at a site that stays occupied.
  • Three quotes were obtained on 2026-08-30. Their amounts are not published here because no official source confirms them; their scopes differ and are not directly comparable (F-02, F-03).

Findings

  • Manufacturer instructions show the moving parts (overflow plate, linkage, drop cylinder) are reachable from the tub side, but the tee and overflow elbow sit inside the framed cavity behind the tub — which is why a fused assembly can only be reached from below or through a new opening.
  • Plumbing plus drywall is two unrelated trades. A B General Building licensee may take that prime contract, or a licensee holding both B and C-36 covers both trades directly; a C-36-only plumber cannot deliver the single-vendor requirement alone.
  • No provider in the shortlist holds a C-9 Drywall classification, so the drywall work will be either self-performed by a B licensee or subcontracted — that must be named in the contract.
  • SF.gov states a permit is required before cutting into or replacing pipes, particularly pipes that will be covered by a wall. Whether this job falls within an exemption is not confirmed in writing by DBI or by any contractor (F-15).
  • If the ceiling is opened, the durable outcome is an access panel rather than a solid patch, so the concealed connections stay reachable; the 12-inch dimension often quoted for that is unverified here (F-07).
  • No retrieved source shows any shortlisted firm has actually done this repair through a ceiling below an occupied unit (F-09). Licences are verified; relevant experience is not.

Recommended actions

  1. Shortlist for the below-access path: Ace Plumbing & Rooter (#829071, B + C-36), We Do Construction (#1096552, B + C-36), Rapid Flow Plumbing & Rooter (#1115649, B + C-36). Ask all three for one written scope covering plumbing, ceiling opening, access panel, patch and paint.
  2. Re-ask the three quoting firms the single question that decides the preferred path: can the overflow plate, linkage and drop cylinder be withdrawn from the tub side, and if the assembly is fused, what is the written plan?
  3. Before any work, get the permit answer in writing: which permit, who pulls it, what it costs, and which inspection is required before the pipes are covered.
  4. Confirm the relationship between Save-On-Interior (#749536) and Bay Area Plumbing (#757796) before treating the combined B + C-36 licence as one vendor's capability (F-13).
  5. Ask every candidate for two references for waste-and-overflow work reached from below, and for occupied-unit work.
  6. Re-check each licence on CSLB on the day the contract is signed, and confirm workers'-comp and liability certificates (two licensees are workers'-comp exempt with no employees).
  7. Fix the tenant logistics in the contract: written notice with date, approximate time and purpose; normal business hours unless the tenant agrees otherwise; dust containment and contents protection in the storage unit; a water shut-off window; leak test before the ceiling closes.
  8. Budget the permit fee ($290 Category 1P) and any off-hour inspection ($555/hour) separately from the vendor's price.
