BATHTUB OVERFLOW / EXECUTIVE SUMMARYResearch snapshot · Nov 7, 2026

PROJECT BRIEF

Executive summary

A decision-ready view of the reported problem, the inquiry strategy and the limits of the evidence.

Situation

Delicate access, occupied building

The requester describes a stuck bathtub overflow trip lever in an occupied San Francisco unit. More than five licensed plumbers were reportedly concerned that an above-access attempt could damage concealed plumbing. The storage space below may provide access through its ceiling. These details have not been independently inspected.

Recommendation

Ask for a staged assessment

Ask first for a non-destructive tub-side assessment. If that is not safe, request an itemized below-access alternative covering protection, opening, plumbing, testing, ceiling restoration and cleanup. Obtain written change-order terms before concealed work.

Sources: Watco: trip-lever bath waste instructions San Francisco DBI: plumbing and mechanical work

Who to ask first

A short, qualified inquiry list

Start with Rapid Flow, Bay Area Plumbing / Save - On - Interior, Heise’s and Ace as inquiry leads. Rapid Flow has a relevant but unverified customer anecdote; Heise’s describes comparable tub waste-and-overflow work. None has confirmed this particular job.

Bay Area Plumbing and Save - On - Interior have separate CSLB licenses; their personnel records name the same sole owner. Confirm which entity would contract and who would perform each trade.

Sources: CSLB #1115649 CSLB #757796 CSLB #749536 CSLB personnel list: Bay Area Plumbing CSLB personnel list: Save - On - Interior Heise’s — drain services and project summaries CSLB #829071

What to request

One written scope, two possible paths

  1. Ask whether an above-tub assessment is appropriate and where the professional would stop if the assembly resists.
  2. Request an itemized alternative for access below, protection, plumbing, testing, ceiling restoration and cleanup.
  3. Name the prime contractor and any subcontractors; confirm current licensing, insurance, permits and inspections for the actual work.
  4. Compare written offers on the same scope and agree on exclusions and change-order terms.
Sources: CSLB: Before hiring a contractor San Francisco DBI: plumbing and mechanical work California Civil Code §1954: entry for repairs
