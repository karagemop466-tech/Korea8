Ten questions to ask every bidder

Ask all ten of all three firms and compare the answers, not the totals.
#QuestionWhy it mattersSource
1Will you attempt extraction through the overflow opening first, and what do you charge if that attempt fails?Separates the cheap attempt from the expensive fallback before anyone commits.S42
2Which legal entity and which licence number will sign the contract?Several firms have more than one licence or a cancelled predecessor at the same address.S03 S04
3Does your price include opening and restoring the storage-unit ceiling, and who does the drywall?Two unrelated trades; the contract must name both licence holders.S18 S19
4Is a permit required for the work you are proposing, and will you pull it?SF.gov states a permit is needed before cutting into pipes covered by a wall.S20 S21
5If the assembly is slip-joint and concealed, will you install a 12-inch access panel instead of a flush patch?Code already requires 12 in. access for concealed slip joints.S33
6Was the building built before 1978, and if so are you an EPA RRP certified firm?Lead-safe work practices may apply to the ceiling cut.S34
7What dust containment and daily cleanup will you use in an occupied building?Tenant safety; requested explicitly by the owner.S31
8Can you work inside 7:00 am - 8:00 pm and give a written arrival window?SF construction hours; unavailable windows are the usual cause of failed access.S31 S30
9What is your warranty on the waste-and-overflow assembly and on the ceiling patch?Two trades means two warranties; get both in the contract.S26
