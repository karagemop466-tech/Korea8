What to do, in order

  1. Start with the cheapest reversible attempt: ask a C-36 contractor to try extracting the linkage through the overflow opening, priced as a separate line item. If it frees, the job is roughly the $135 repair quote.
  2. Do not authorise a ceiling cut until the access route is agreed in writing. Opening the storage-unit ceiling below reaches the trap tee that overflow-side work cannot, but it triggers a permit under SF.gov's own wording and needs a drywall trade.
  3. Best single-provider fit: Ace Plumbing & Rooter, CSLB #829071 — the only shortlisted firm holding both the C-36 plumbing and B general building classifications, so one licensed entity can own both trades.
  4. Every dollar figure in the brief is user-reported and undocumented. The spread from $135 to $614 is a scope difference, not a pricing difference; the three quotes are not comparable.
  5. One constraint supplied by the owner is deliberately absent from this site and from every file in the repository. The official permit rules are published instead, so the decision can be made privately.

The decision that actually matters

From above, or from below?

There are two access routes and they are not equally reversible. Through the overflow opening costs almost nothing to try and opens no wall. Through the storage-unit ceiling below reaches the trap tee that overflow-side work cannot reach, but SF.gov states a permit is needed before cutting into or replacing pipes, particularly pipes that will be covered by a wall (S20), and it needs a drywall trade on top of the plumber.
