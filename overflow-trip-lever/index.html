Executive summary

Problem. A bath-tub waste-and-overflow trip lever (the lever on the overflow plate that raises/lowers a drop cylinder — the “plunger” — inside the hidden overflow tee) is stuck in plumbing concealed behind the tub wall. The unit is tenant-occupied; the space below is a storage unit, so below-access would mean opening its drywall ceiling.

What is verified. Watco’s manufacturer instructions show the overflow plate, linkage and drop cylinder are serviced through the overflow opening from inside the tub (steps 5–7) — so a from-above attempt is the documented first move for those parts, without forcing anything that could damage the hidden tee/elbow (RULE-07). If the hidden tee/overflow elbow itself must be replaced, SF.gov states a permit is needed before cutting into or replacing pipes that will be covered by a wall, and a City-registered licensed contractor can obtain it online (Express Permit where eligible) (RULE-01/02).

Who can do it all-in-one. California law lets a B (General Building) contractor prime a plumbing + drywall job when it involves two unrelated trades, when it also holds C-36, or when it subcontracts to a licensed C-36 (BPC §7057(b)). On CSLB records checked 2026-11-07, Save-On-Interior #749536 (B+C-36, same address/phone as Bay Area Plumbing #757796), Rapid Flow #1115649 (B+C-36), Ace Plumbing & Rooter #829071 (B+C-36 + others) and Ayoob & Peery #392967 (B+C-36, licensed since 1980) structurally fit the all-in-one requirement. None has been contacted; none has confirmed this exact job — those cells stay blank (F-03).

Costs. Tenant protection. Civil Code §1954: reasonable written notice (24 hours presumed reasonable) stating date, approximate time and purpose; normal business hours unless the tenant consents otherwise (RQ-08).
