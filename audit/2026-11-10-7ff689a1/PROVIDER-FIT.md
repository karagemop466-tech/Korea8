## 1. The mechanical fact that decides the shortlist

Read from the manufacturer's own instruction sheet ([Watco 500/501/600-TL bath waste](https://www.watcomfg.com/watco/Instructions/500-501-600TL_Instructions_Bath_Waste_Sheet.pdf)):

| Path | What it reaches | Access needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Overflow plate + screws, drop cylinder, trip linkage, QUICK ADJUST linkage nut, SLIP LOCK / TURNSTYLE engagement | the lever mechanism itself, the part that is stuck | **inside the tub, from above** |
| strainer body seating, drain elbow, **overflow elbow, tee, trap arms, solvent-weld joints** | the waste-and-overflow assembly and its connections | **below or behind the tub** |
| tub-side slip-joint reconnection to the tee | the final connection | under the tub, hand access |

Consequence: a plumber who can free, re-seat or re-adjust the lever has a legitimate from-above repair. A plumber who says the waste and overflow must be **replaced** is describing a job that is physically not a from-above job on this design. Both of those statements appear in the owner's quotes — the $614 and $550 replacement scopes versus the $135 "repair or adjust" scope. **The register's own tub-side-first requirement (T-03) is sound; the manufacturer sheet is what proves it.**

## 2. Where the licence actually permits one firm to do both trades

| Provision | Text (verbatim, current) | Effect on this job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [BPC 7059(a)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=7059) | "Nothing contained in this section shall prohibit a specialty contractor from taking and executing a contract involving the use of two or more crafts or trades, if the performance of the work in the crafts or trades, other than in which they are licensed, is **incidental and supplemental** to the performance of the work in the craft for which the specialty contractor is licensed." | A C-36-only plumber may lawfully include a small access-hole patch inside the plumbing contract. |
| same section, first paragraph | "the board **may restrict** the scope of any such activities" | what "incidental" means is the board's to define; a firm that makes it a separately priced line item is no longer clearly incidental. |
| [16 CCR Div. 8 Art. 3, C-9](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/About_Us/Library/Licensing_Classifications/Licensing_Classifications_Detail.aspx?Class=C-9) | "…performs the **taping and texturing** operations including the application of compounds that adhere to wall board to produce a continuous smooth or textured surface" | texture matching is C-9 work — the reason a patch-and-blend ceiling wants a drywall licence if it is priced on its own. |
| [16 CCR Div. 8 Art. 3, C-36](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/About_Us/Library/Licensing_Classifications/Licensing_Classifications_Detail.aspx?Class=C36) | water supply, drainage, piping, venting, gas appliances, plus "installing, repair and maintenance work in connection with" those systems | trip-lever, drop cylinder, waste and overflow, trap work sit squarely inside it. |
| [CSLB Fast Facts, "B"](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Resources/Contractors/FastFacts-GeneralB.pdf) | "Enter a prime or sub-contract for **two or more separate and unrelated trades** and self-perform the work (framing and carpentry cannot count as one of the trades)" | B + C-36 firms (6 in the register) may hold one contract covering plumbing **and** the ceiling, because two unrelated trades are involved. |

**So the owner's "all-in-one" requirement is satisfiable two ways, and only one of them needs a general licence:** one prime contract from a B + C-36 holder, or a C-36 plumber whose patch is genuinely incidental. That is the finding missing from the canonical register (handoff request 7FF-E-1).

## 6. Questions to ask, in the order that costs least

1. **"Is the lever free, or is the assembly the fault?"** A plumber who can answer without opening anything has not looked. The Watco sheet lets them work the drop cylinder and linkage from the overflow opening first ([steps 6–7, QUICK ADJUST / SLIP LOCK / TURNSTYLE](https://www.watcomfg.com/watco/Instructions/500-501-600TL_Instructions_Bath_Waste_Sheet.pdf)).
2. **"Will you price the tub-side attempt separately from the replacement?"** Then the $135-style investigation is the cheapest real option, and the $550–614 replacement only becomes a commitment if the assembly itself is failed.
3. **"Who closes the ceiling, and under what licence?"** Accept either "my B (or C-9) licence, same contract" or "the patch is incidental to the plumbing work under BPC 7059(a)". Treat "a drywall guy will come later, you arrange it" as two contracts and two chances for the water-damage conversation to be lost.
4. **"What do you do about the storage unit underneath?"** Written entry notice, protective covering, and who pays if belongings get wet. The register's tenanted-environment criterion is C3; only Ace and Fast Response publish HOA / property-manager workflows.
5. **"Has the ceiling been wet, and does it need drying before it is closed?"** Wet board closed over a damp cavity is the failure mode; a firm that mentions drying or moisture checks (24/7 Rooter sells water restoration; Heise's advertises water-stain ceiling patching) is thinking about it.
6. **"Which number dispatches your crew?"** Rapid Flow, 24/7 Rooter and Heise's each publish a contact detail that differs from the board record (toll-free vs local, a second storefront number, an Evans Ave registration vs an Ocean Ave storefront). Confirm the line you will use at 6 a.m.
7. **"Show me the permit or the exemption you're relying on."** Neutral, and it is the cheapest protection available: the City requires a plumbing permit before **cutting into or replacing pipes** "particularly pipes that will be covered by a wall", and inspection **before covering pipes** ([sf.gov service page](https://www.sf.gov/apply-plumbing-and-mechanical-permit)). SF Plumbing Code 104.2 lists what is exempt — leak repair, trap clearing and faucet work that do **not** require cutting or removing piping; replacing a bath waste carries the issuance-fee carve-out in 104.5 only "**without a change in drainage, piping or venting**".
8. **"If the drop cylinder is the only failed part, can you source just that?"** The Watco family is serviceable part-by-part (overflow plate screws, drop cylinder, linkage), so a whole-assembly quote should be justified by what is actually corroded.

## 7. Bottom line

Buy the tub-side diagnosis first from a licensed C-36 plumber (Fast Response's $135 scope is the one that matches it; Heise's advertises the overflow-plate work itself). If the assembly must be replaced, the decision is not "plumber vs drywaller" but **who holds the second trade**: Ace and Rapid Flow each carry **B + C-36** in one licence, Heise's has the most directly comparable published jobs and an advertised access-hole patching service but relies on the 7059(a) incidental allowance for that patch, and Bernal Hill (C-9 + B) is the texture-matching partner if restoration is priced separately.
