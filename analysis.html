A-06A San Francisco permit is required before pipe is cut or covered — check the §104.2 exemptionDBI's official page says a permit is needed before cutting into or replacing pipes, particularly pipes that will be covered by a wall, and points to the exemption list in SF Plumbing Code §104.2. A waste-and-overflow replacement is exactly that kind of pipe work. The same page lists the licence classes that may apply (A; B after scope approval; C4/C36; C16; C20; C54), and notes that contractors registered for Express Permits on PermitSF skip staff review. The permit determination for this specific scope has not been obtained — ask the Permit Center help desk, 628-652-4900.verified (official source)sf.gov — Apply for a plumbing and mechanical permit (DBI)

A-08Removal from above is the manufacturer's documented service pathThe manufacturer's installation sheet for a trip-lever bath waste reaches the linkage and drop cylinder through the overflow opening — its own service step is to remove the overflow plate and lift out the cylinder assembly. Nothing in the documented sequence requires opening a wall. That makes the 'from above' attempt worth re-asking, even though the requester reports five or more plumbers declined it.verified (official source)Watco — Trip Lever Bath Waste installation instructions (manufacturer PDF)
A-09If the ceiling is opened, the code asks for an access openingThe California Plumbing Code requires access to concealed slip-joint connections (12-inch minimum access opening; CPC §402.10 in the 2022 edition, renumbered from §404.2 in the 2010 edition). Patching the ceiling solid and refusing an access panel creates the same access problem again for the next repair.verified (official source)IAPMO — UPC chapter 4, §402.10 (code publisher)
