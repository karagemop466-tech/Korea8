1. When a plumbing permit is required (SF.gov / DBI)

  • Per SF.gov, a plumbing permit is required before cutting into or replacing pipes, particularly pipes that will be covered by a wall or buried in the ground.
  • This job involves concealed waste-and-overflow plumbing behind the tub wall, and possibly pipe work behind the ceiling of the storage unit below — both fall squarely within permit-required scope if pipes are cut or replaced.
  • Applying online: PermitSF — plumbing and mechanical permit.
  • Permit Center help desk: 628-652-4900 · 49 South Van Ness, 2nd floor · permitsf@sfgov.org.
  • A plumbing/mechanical permit applicant must generally hold a CSLB license of class C4, C16, C20, C36, C54, B, or A; C-36 is the plumbing classification. Scope may be further limited by license type.
  • Work must be inspected before pipes are covered.

Source: SF.gov — Apply for a plumbing and mechanical permit

2. Work that does NOT require a permit — SF Plumbing Code §104.2 exemptions

Per the San Francisco Plumbing Code (via SF code library), a permit shall NOT be required only for:

  • Repair of leaks not requiring cutting into or removing piping;
  • Unstopping of traps, sewers, vents or waste pipes not requiring cutting into or removal of traps or piping;
  • Replacement, repair or maintenance of faucets, angle stops, hose bibbs or roof drains not requiring cutting into or removal of piping;
  • Replacement of toilets (except in structures with alternate water piping);
  • Replacement of furnace filters.
Application to this job: Replacing a bathtub waste-and-overflow assembly or trip lever plumbing behind a wall requires cutting into/removing piping — it is not exempt. Only trivial trim-level work (e.g., replacing the face plate or adjusting linkage without cutting pipe) could fall within an exemption. A licensed plumber should determine the actual scope before any work begins.

Sources: SF Building/Plumbing Code — §104.0 Permits (SF code library) · 2025 San Francisco Plumbing Code (official PDF)

3. Kitchen/bath remodel permits (context if drywall/wall work expands)

  • A kitchen or bath remodel permit is required before renovating or replacing fixtures; online application suits R-3 residential or R-2 apartment units with no wall removal/moving (no plans).
  • Minimum valuations per DBI cost schedule: $24,663 per kitchen, $20,552 per bathroom (reference only; small repairs are typically handled via trade permits instead).
  • For R-2 apartment buildings, property owners may do sheetrock/drywall/cabinetry themselves but must hire contractors for electrical or plumbing work.
  • Over-the-counter (OTC) permits are available for qualifying smaller interior residential remodels.

Source: SF.gov — Apply for a kitchen or bath remodel permit · SF.gov — Over-the-Counter permit guide

4. Client position on permits

Internal use only. The client has elected to have the work performed without a permit (permit decision reasons are private and are intentionally not recorded here). The client wants a licensed plumber or contractor. Because concealed pipe work generally requires a plumbing permit pulled by a licensed C-36 (or other qualifying) contractor, the selected professional should confirm permit requirements with SFDBI before starting work, and the client should understand any risk of proceeding unpermitted.
