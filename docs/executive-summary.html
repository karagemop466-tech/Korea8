1. The problem

The bathtub overflow trip lever (plunger-style waste-and-overflow stopper control) is stuck inside the plumbing concealed behind the bathtub wall. The overflow is in a hard-to-access area, so reaching the plumbing may require opening drywall.

  • Five or more licensed plumbers have been consulted; all were unable or unwilling to remove the overflow without concern about destroying hidden plumbing behind the wall.
  • Preferred approach (client): remove or repair the overflow from above, avoiding wall demolition.
  • Fallback approach (all plumbers consulted): work from below. Because a storage unit occupies the space below the tub, this may require opening the drywall ceiling of the unit below.
  • The client needs an all-in-one service: plumbing plus drywall (cut, repair, patch, paint of any ceiling/wall opened).
  • The unit is tenant-occupied: the contractor must perform delicate work in a sensitive environment with coordinated tenant access.
  • The client wants a licensed plumber or contractor (California CSLB C-36 plumbing contractor, or B general building contractor for combined plumbing/drywall scope).

4. Permits and regulations (San Francisco)

  • SF.gov (Department of Building Inspection): a plumbing permit is required before cutting into or replacing pipes, particularly pipes that will be covered by a wall or buried in the ground — which is exactly the situation here (concealed waste-and-overflow plumbing).
  • SF Plumbing Code §104.2 (exempt work — no permit required) covers only: repair of leaks not requiring cutting into/removing piping; unstopping traps/sewers/vents/waste pipes without cutting; replacement/repair/maintenance of faucets, angle stops, hose bibbs or roof drains without cutting piping; toilet replacement; furnace filter replacement. Replacing a waste-and-overflow assembly behind a wall is not exempt.
  • A plumbing/mechanical permit applicant must generally hold a C4, C16, C20, C36, C54, B, or A CSLB license; C36 is the plumbing classification.
  • Permits are filed via PermitSF (online); Permit Center help desk: 628-652-4900, 49 South Van Ness, 2nd floor.
  • Client position: the work is requested without a permit (internal client decision; reasons private and not disclosed here). A licensed professional should assess whether the planned scope legally requires a permit before work begins.

See permits.html with official links.
