Finding B — Service model needed: plumbing + drywall all-in-one

  • Below-access work opens the drywall ceiling below (storage unit), so the contractor must repair/paint drywall after plumbing work — a combined plumbing + drywall scope.
  • A California B (general building) contractor can cover general building scope; a C-36 plumbing contractor covers plumbing. Drywall-only work may fall under a different classification; confirm with the contractor and CSLB classifications.
  • Alternatively, hire a plumbing contractor plus a separate drywall contractor — but the client prefers all-in-one.

UNVERIFIED — licensing boundaries between C-36, B, and drywall classifications for this specific scope should be confirmed with the contractor/CSLB.

Finding E — Permit exposure

  • Concealed pipe work (cutting/replacing piping behind walls/ceilings) generally requires a San Francisco plumbing permit pulled by a licensed contractor (C-36 or other qualifying class).
  • SF Plumbing Code §104.2 exemptions do not cover work requiring cutting into/removing piping, so a full waste-and-overflow replacement is not exempt.
  • The client has elected not to pull a permit this week (internal decision; reasons private, not recorded here). The selected licensed professional must confirm permit obligations with SFDBI before work begins.

Finding F — Tenant-occupied, sensitive environment

  • The unit is occupied by tenants; access must be coordinated and work performed delicately.
  • If below-access is required, the storage unit below is also affected — ceiling drywall opening, dust, and access to that space must be managed with its occupants/owner.
  • Screen contractors explicitly for experience working in occupied units.
