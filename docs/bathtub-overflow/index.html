Executive summary

The tub waste-and-overflow trip lever is stuck in concealed plumbing. Multiple plumbers reportedly declined to remove it from above because of potential damage to hidden piping. Above-access removal is the preferred first option only if the plumber can explain a safe method; otherwise, access from the storage-room ceiling below may be necessary. The preferred engagement is a licensed plumbing contractor who can coordinate both plumbing and drywall restoration, protect tenant privacy and contents, and give a written access/repair plan before opening the ceiling.

Research status: No provider has been independently confirmed as meeting the full plumbing + drywall + licensing + relevant-work criteria. Use the listed directories as lead sources, then verify license and exact scope directly.

Recommended procurement approach

  1. Request a site assessment and explicitly describe the stuck trip lever, prior failed/cautious attempts, concealed piping, occupied unit, and potential ceiling access.
  2. Ask for two options in writing: tub-side approach (method, limitations, stop point) and controlled access from below (opening size/location, dust/water protection, plumbing repair, drywall patch/texture/paint responsibility).
  3. Verify the applicable California license and status through CSLB; confirm insurance, who performs drywall, schedule, warranty, and written authorization before work.
  4. Do not authorize destructive access until tenant coordination, storage contents protection, and any required building/permit approvals are resolved.
