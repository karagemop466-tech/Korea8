Method and scope limits

  • Step 1 - Licence screen. Each candidate was checked against its own CSLB licence-detail page, retrieved directly on 2026-11-08. The licence number, legal entity, classifications, expiry, status and any additional status were transcribed; a provider whose licence is suspended, or whose licence could not be found, is excluded from the ranking instead of being ranked low (see excluded_rows).
  • Step 2 - Scope screen. A combined plumbing-plus-restoration job needs both trades lawfully covered. Under Business and Professions Code s.7057(b) a B licensee may take a prime contract for other trades only when the contract requires two unrelated trades, or the licensee holds the classification, or it subcontracts an appropriately licensed contractor. So a B + C-36 licence supports a combined prime contract, but it does not by itself prove who performs the drywall; the named restoration party must still be confirmed in writing.
  • Step 3 - Evidence screen. Providers were ordered by the strength of published, checkable evidence directly about this kind of work, not by marketing claims: a company-published case of the same assembly replaced through ceiling access outranks a general statement that all work is handled.
  • Step 4 - Estimates are not ranked as prices. The three owner-supplied amounts describe different scopes (replacement; lever/plug/spout with diverter; adjustment only). They are shown as owner-reported context, never as verified prices, and they were not used to reorder the ranking.
  • Step 5 - Unknowns stay blank. Availability, exact-job acceptance, ceiling-restoration scope, review ratings and verified prices are unconfirmed for every row and are left blank rather than estimated.

What would change this ranking

  1. A written combined quote from any Tier B plumbing lead that names a C-9 restoration party would move it above the current tier.
  2. Confirmation that a provider will not take a single-fixture repair would drop it to the registry pool.
  3. A change in CSLB status, or a new complaint disclosure, would trigger immediate re-ranking; status was current as of 2026-11-08.
  4. Verified review evidence, if it becomes retrievable, has not been used at all in this ordering.

Scope note: This ranking covers licensed contractors that could lawfully perform a concealed bathtub waste-and-overflow trip-lever repair in a tenant-occupied San Francisco unit, with the plumbing and the ceiling-restoration trades named under one contract. It is an inquiry priority order, not a competence guarantee, not a booking and not a price comparison. No provider was contacted for this analysis.
