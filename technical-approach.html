T-01How a trip lever worksA bathtub trip-lever assembly consists of an overflow faceplate with a lever, a brass linkage inside the overflow pipe, and a plunger/drop-cylinder that seats in the drain passage. Lever up = open (drain), lever down = closed (fill).This Old House — How To Replace a Tub Drain
T-02Adjust vs replaceA stuck or slow trip lever is first addressed by adjusting/cleaning the linkage; a corroded or fused assembly typically ends in full waste-and-overflow (W&O) replacement. The brief's 5+ plumbers declining removal-from-above is consistent with a fused/corroded assembly rather than a simple linkage adjustment.This Old House (adjust steps); r/Plumbing stuck trip-lever thread (anecdotal)
T-03Why access from below is the standard fallbackManufacturer installation instructions show the W&O assembly is secured with a retainer plate and screws under/behind the tub, with the overflow elbow set against the tub wall inside the framing cavity — i.e., the assembly physically lives in the hidden cavity behind/below the tub. When the internals are fused, replacement generally requires reaching that cavity: from above via an access opening, or from below through the ceiling of the space underneath. (URL updated 2026-11-07: Watco reorganized its instruction library and the former PDF path now 404s; the current official instructions page is linked.)Watco Manufacturing — official Trip Lever Installation Instructions (Quick Adjust / Slip Lock / TurnStyle)
T-04Code — access openingsThe California Plumbing Code (2022 §402.10; numbered §404.2 in the 2010 edition) requires fixtures with concealed slip-joint connections to be provided with an access panel or utility space of at least 12 inches in the least dimension, unobstructed, so connections are inspectable and repairable. Tub W&O assemblies commonly use slip joints — so when the ceiling is opened below, fitting a proper access panel (rather than a solid patch) is the code-aligned outcome. (Link updated 2026-11-07: IAPMO's 'Answers and Analysis' page is now members-only; the official 2025 CPC epub is linked instead, and the 12-inch §402.10 wording is confirmed in the linked 2022 CPC/UPC reprint. Section numbering varies by edition — see flag F-07.)IAPMO — 2025 California Plumbing Code (official epub) + CPC 2022 §402.10 reprint
T-05Code — tub waste and overflow sizingBathtubs must have a waste outlet of at least 1-1/2 inches equipped with a watertight stopper; where an overflow is installed it must also be at least 1-1/2 inches, and the waste must be arranged so standing water does not rise in the overflow when the stopper is closed. Any replacement assembly must meet these dimensions — standard trip-lever kits comply.CPC/UPC bathtub waste outlets & overflows (UpCodes reprint; IAPMO/CPC arrangement rule)
T-06Licensing — who can legally do plumbing + drywall in one jobPer CSLB's official guidance, a C-36 contractor may contract only plumbing work; a B General Building contractor is the appropriate prime when a job requires more than two types of work (e.g., plumbing + drywall), and a B cannot legally contract a job involving only one trade. Therefore the brief's all-in-one requirement maps to: B-licensed GC prime (hiring C-36 plumbing + C-9 drywall subs), or a license combining B + C-36 (e.g., Ace #829071, We Do Construction #1096552) with a drywall sub for the ceiling.CSLB — Find A Licensed Contractor / License Classifications (official)
T-07Cost contextPublished 2026 third-party ranges (context only, not official): bathtub plumbing repairs $100–$700+ (HomeGuide); tub drain replacement ~$700 with easy access, $500–$1,025 with difficult access (Angi); drywall patching adds $300–$930 (Angi). The three owner-recorded quotes (amounts on file, not published — F-02) cover an adjust scope, a lever/diverter replacement, and a full W&O replacement; a below-access ceiling opening + patch is additional and not yet quoted by anyone.HomeGuide (2026) + Angi (Oct 2026) — third-party cost guides
T-08Occupied-unit logisticsCeiling opening in the storage unit below an occupied unit implies: tenant notice and scheduling around the unit above, dust containment in the storage unit, contents protection/relocation, water shut-off windows, and leak-testing the new assembly before closing the ceiling. Providers documenting property-manager/HOA programs and occupied-space work (Ace, Heise's, SF Building Group, Bay Area Plumbing) are structurally familiar with these constraints.Provider sites: Ace (HOA/property managers), SF Building Group (occupied-space off-hours work), Bay Area Plumbing (landlord/HOA 24-7), Heise's (property maintenance division)
