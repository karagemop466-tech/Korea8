Technical Approach

How a trip-lever bath waste is built, what each access route costs, and which code clause applies.

Each finding is either a direct quotation from a manufacturer or regulator, or a labelled analysis that follows from those quotations. Manufacturer documentation describes a typical trip-lever waste; the actual assembly in this unit has not been inspected, so the parts list may differ.

Two access routes, and what decides between them

Route A — from the tub

Overflow plate off, assembly out through the opening. No wall opened, no drywall, no make-good, and the cheapest possible outcome. It is the manufacturer's own service path (T-01, T-02). It fails if the linkage is kinked or the drop cylinder is seized in the tee (T-04).

Route B — from below

Open the storage-unit ceiling, reach the waste-and-overflow, then close it. Requires a second trade, a tenant notice for a second space, and a decision on the ceiling detail: a solid patch or a framed access panel (T-05 to T-07).

Findings

Fact

The whole working mechanism hangs off the overflow plate

On a typical trip-lever bath waste the overflow plate is held by two screws, and the drop-cylinder assembly and brass linkage hang from that plate down into the overflow tee.

'Install overflow plate and drop cylinder assembly with overflow plate screws.'

Watco Manufacturing, Trip Lever Installation Instructions (models 500/501/600/650-TL), step 6 · manufacturer

Fact

The manufacturer's own service step is to remove the plate and pull the assembly

Watco's adjustment step tells the installer to remove the overflow plate and drop-cylinder assembly and readjust them — i.e. the assembly is designed to be serviced from inside the tub without opening the wall.

'If necessary, remove overflow plate and drop cylinder assembly and readjust to ensure drain stops water when in the closed position.'

Watco Manufacturing, Trip Lever Installation Instructions, step 7 · manufacturer

Fact

The connection behind the tub is a slip-joint assembly on this product family

Watco describes the 501/502 trip-lever bath waste as a 'Sturdy 1.5" 17g and 20g Tubular Brass Slip-Joint Assembly', with a retainer plate for the overflow elbow and brass slip nuts and polywashers joining the tee.

'Sturdy 1.5" 17g and 20g Tubular Brass Slip-Joint Assembly'

Watco 501/502/551/552 series bath waste specification sheet · manufacturer

Analysis

Why removal from above can fail without the plumber being wrong

The above-access route depends on the plate screws backing out and the drop cylinder sliding free of the tee. If the linkage is kinked, the cylinder is seized in the tee, or the screws spin in a corroded overflow elbow, the only remaining way to reach the joint is through the wall or ceiling. That is consistent with the reported outcome — five or more plumbers declining to force it — and it means 'can it come out from above?' is a physical question that only an on-site attempt can answer. No provider has yet given a written yes or no (F-03).

Analysis of T-01 to T-03 plus the requester's account of five or more refusals · analysis

Fact

A concealed slip joint needs a 12-inch access panel

If the waste-and-overflow behind the wall contains a slip joint — as the tubular-brass assemblies in T-03 do — the code requires an access panel or utility space of at least 12 inches in its least dimension, unobstructed, so the joint can be reached for repair.

'Fixtures having concealed slip joint connections shall be provided with an access panel or utility space not less than 12 inches (305 mm) in its least dimension and so arranged without obstructions as to make such connections accessible for inspection and repair.'

Uniform Plumbing Code §402.10, quoted by IAPMO Answers & Analysis, chapter 4 · code-guidance

Fact

But the panel rule turns on the joint type, not on the repair

IAPMO's explanation is explicit that the requirement exists because a slip-joint washer can fail, and that two-piece assemblies and soldered tubing joints are not slip joints, so they do not trigger the panel requirement.

'Two-piece assemblies are not slip joints by definition… Therefore, no access panel is required. The same applies when tubing joints are assembled by soldering.'

IAPMO Code Spotlight, '402.10 Slip Joint Connections' · code-guidance

Analysis

What this means for the ceiling

A solid drywall patch is defensible only if the concealed assembly is soldered or otherwise not a slip joint. If it is the usual tubular-brass slip-joint waste, closing the ceiling without an access opening leaves a concealed slip joint that the code says must be accessible (T-05, T-06). The practical answer that satisfies both readings is a framed 12-inch access panel rather than a solid patch: it complies whichever joint type is found, and it makes the next service call cheaper. That is a recommendation, not a code requirement.

Analysis of T-05 and T-06 · analysis

Fact

Plumbing permits name a small set of licence types

DBI lists A, B (subject to approval based on scope), C-4, C-36, C-16 (sprinkler), C-20 (mechanical) and C-54 (shower pan only) as the licence types that may apply for a plumbing or mechanical permit. B-2 Residential Remodeling is absent from that list.

'C4 or C36 license for a plumbing permit' / 'B license, after approval based on the scope of work'

SF.gov — Apply for a plumbing and mechanical permit · official-regulator

Fact

One licence can cover both trades only if it holds both, or subcontracts one

BPC §7057(b) lets a B contractor take a prime contract involving other trades when the contract requires at least two unrelated trades, or the contractor holds the classification, or it subcontracts an appropriately licensed contractor. A firm holding B + C-36 has both trades in house; a B-only firm has to subcontract the plumbing.

'…unless the prime contract requires at least two unrelated building trades or crafts other than framing or carpentry, or unless the general building contractor holds the appropriate license classification or subcontracts with an appropriately licensed contractor to perform the work.'

California Business and Professions Code §7057(b) · statute

Fact

Tenant notice has a documented minimum

Entry to make agreed repairs requires reasonable written notice stating date, approximate time and purpose, during normal business hours, with 24 hours presumed reasonable. A plumbing visit to the unit above and a drywall visit to the storage unit below are two entries.

'The notice shall include the date, approximate time, and purpose of the entry… Twenty-four hours shall be presumed to be reasonable notice in absence of evidence to the contrary.'

California Civil Code §1954(d)(1) · statute

Fact

Where the 2026 licence threshold sits

The exemption for small jobs is now $1,000 and, by its own wording, is unavailable where the work needs a building permit. A plumbing permit requirement therefore removes the exemption regardless of price.

'…the aggregate contract price for labor, materials, and all other items is less than one thousand dollars ($1,000)… and the work or operation does not require a building permit.'

California Business and Professions Code §7048(a), as amended by AB 1170 (Stats. 2025, Ch. 67), effective 1 January 2026 · statute

The clause that decides the ceiling

UPC §402.10 requires an access panel of at least 12 inches in its least dimension where a slip joint is concealed. IAPMO explains that soldered and two-piece joints are not slip joints, so they do not trigger the requirement. Which applies here is unknown until the assembly is opened — F-16. A framed access panel therefore satisfies the code on either finding, while a solid patch only satisfies it on one.
