The assembly

What a trip-lever bath waste is, how it is meant to be serviced, and why access is the whole problem.

Everything on this page that is marked verified comes from the manufacturer's own trip-lever instruction sheet, linked on each row. Access options A and B come from the project brief and are marked unverified accordingly.

T-01What the part isA trip-lever bath waste is a waste-and-overflow assembly whose stopper is not in the drain. The visible control is a lever on the overflow cover plate on the tub wall; the lever is connected by a linkage to a drop cylinder (plunger) that travels inside the overflow tube and seats in the tee to close the drain.
Screw / Linkage / Overflow Plate / Overflow Plate Screws / Linkage Nut ... Step 6. Install overflow plate and drop cylinder assembly with overflow plate screws. ... Make sure brass linkage is not kinked

T-02Parts behind the plateManufacturer documentation names these components: overflow plate, overflow plate screws, linkage, linkage nut, drop cylinder, retainer plate, overflow elbow, tee, and the screws that hold the retainer plate against the interior tub wall.
Step 5. From inside the tub, start the two retainer plate screws in the bottom two holes in the overflow elbow. Hang the retainer plate over the screw heads and tighten the screws until the retainer plate makes contact with the interior tub wall and overflow gasket is tight against the exterior tub wall.

T-03Why it is accessible from above in principleThe manufacturer's own procedure reaches the whole working mechanism through the overflow opening: the drop cylinder assembly is inserted into and removed from the overflow, and the retainer plate is worked from inside the tub. Nothing in the installation sequence requires opening the wall, so an extraction from above is the designed service path.
Insert drop-cylinder assembly into overflow with trip lever in down (open) position. Screw overflow plate onto overflow drain.

T-04Orientation constraint that matters during reassemblyOn Schedule 40 trip-lever bath wastes the arrow on the tee must point down, and solvent cement must not enter the tee cavity. Getting either wrong during a replacement from below is a classic cause of a reassembly that never seals.
IMPORTANT! On Schedule 40 trip lever bath wastes, the arrow on tee must be pointing down. Do not allow solvent cement to enter tee cavity.

T-05Adjustment is a first-class repair, not a workaroundThe manufacturer's documented procedure is to remove the plate and drop cylinder assembly and readjust it until the drain seals; a slow drip from inside the tee is described as normal and acceptable. An adjustment-only visit is therefore a legitimate scope, which is what the $135 quote describes.
Step 7. If necessary, remove overflow plate and drop cylinder assembly and readjust to ensure drain stops water when in the closed position. The drop cylinder should bottom out inside the tee slightly before the fully-closed position. A slow drip from inside the tee is normal and acceptable.

T-06Acceptance criterion after serviceThe manufacturer's stated acceptance condition is that the drain stops water in the closed position, with the drop cylinder bottoming out in the tee slightly before fully closed, and that a slow drip from inside the tee is normal and acceptable. Use this as the written sign-off test — it stops a contractor declaring 'done' while the tub still will not hold water, and it stops a slow drip being treated as a defect.
If necessary, remove overflow plate and drop cylinder assembly and readjust to ensure drain stops water when in the closed position. The drop cylinder should bottom out inside the tee slightly before the fully-closed position. A slow drip from inside the tee is normal and acceptable.

The instruction sheet also warns: 'DO NOT USE OIL-BASED PUTTY PRODUCTS: Watco does not recommend and does not warranty this product when oil/petroleum based products are used when installing this product.' Worth putting in the work order.

T-09Access option B — from behind the wall / belowRequires opening finished surfaces (wall behind the tub, or the ceiling of the storage unit below) to reach the concealed waste-and-overflow piping, then patching and finishing. This is why the provider must hold both a plumbing classification and a general-building/drywall classification, or subcontract the second trade.

Cross-reference R-04: with a permit in place, DBI requires the plumbing inspection BEFORE the pipes are covered. Sequencing the drywall patch before the inspection would defeat the inspection.

T-10Why one licence is not always enoughCSLB issues plumbing as a specialty C-36 classification. A contractor holding only C-36 is not licensed to contract for the general-building/drywall scope, so an all-in-one job either uses a licence that carries both classifications or pairs a plumber with a second licensed contractor.
