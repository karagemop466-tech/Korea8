## Recommendations
1. Treat the three quotes as scope-differentiated, not comparable: an adjust/repair scope versus a lever-and-diverter replacement versus a full waste-and-overflow replacement. Amounts are on file with the owner and are not published (F-02). None includes the likely ceiling opening/patch.
2. For the preferred outcome (removal from above), re-engage the three quoting companies with the exact question: can the overflow faceplate and fused internals be extracted from the tub side without opening the wall — and get the answer in writing.
3. For the fallback (below-floor access), shortlist the multi-trade-licensed firms — Ace Plumbing & Rooter (B + C-36, 25+ years, HOA/property-manager experience), We Do Construction (B + C-36, self-performed plumbing under one GC contract), Rapid Flow (B + C-36, GC positioning, 24/7), and SF Building Group (B, occupied-space off-hours work) — or a B-2 Residential Remodeler route (24/7 SF Handyman & Remodeling, #1105133). Require the drywall scope (open, frame, fit access panel, patch, texture) in the same contract.
4. Whichever vendor opens the ceiling, require a code-aligned access panel (CPC 402.10: 12-inch minimum access for concealed slip-joint connections) rather than a solid patch, so future service does not require re-opening.
5. Before signing: click each provider's CSLB link to re-verify status, confirm workers'-compensation and liability insurance certificates (three providers are WC-exempt no-employee operations: Bay Area Plumbing, SF Building Group, Rapid Flow), and confirm tenant-notice/dust-containment logistics in writing.
6. Confirm the matched identity of the three quoting companies (generic names) before scheduling — see Flagged for Review F-02.
