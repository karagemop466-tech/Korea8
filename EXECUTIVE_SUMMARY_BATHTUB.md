## 4. Code and permit context (official sources)

- **California Plumbing Code (2025, CCR Title 24 Part 5), §404.2 Overflows:** where a fixture is provided with an overflow, the waste must be arranged so standing water cannot rise in the overflow with the stopper closed or remain in it when empty; the overflow pipe connects to the house/inlet side of the fixture trap. Concealed waste-and-overflow fittings (typical tub installations) must be drainage-approved materials. Source: epubs.iapmo.org/2025/CPC/ (official IAPMO e-publication); language also quoted in IAPMO Uniform Codes Spotlight.
- **Contractor license:** California requires a CSLB Class C-36 plumbing license for plumbing work valued at $500 or more (labor + materials). Verify at cslb.ca.gov.
- **San Francisco plumbing permit:** SF Building Code §104.0 exempts minor repairs (e.g., faucet/angle-stop/hose-bib/roof-drain repair not requiring cutting into or removal of piping). Work requiring cutting into or removing piping — which opening the concealed overflow plumbing likely involves — is outside that exemption and likely requires a plumbing permit. SF DBI requires plumbing contractors to hold a C4/C36 California license, SF business license, workers' comp, and DBI registration for permits (sfdbi.org).
- **Permit status for this job: undetermined.** Confirm with SF DBI and the hired contractor before work.
