## 1. The problem (verbatim from the request)

The bathtub overflow (trip-lever waste-and-overflow assembly) is in a hard-to-access
area and is stuck inside the hidden plumbing behind the wall. The trip lever cannot be
removed from above without concern about destroying the hidden plumbing behind the wall:
**more than 5 licensed plumbers** have been unable to remove it from above. All of them
recommend working from below, where there is a **storage unit below** the tub; accessing
the hidden plumbing may require **opening the drywall ceiling** in that storage unit.
The unit is **occupied by tenants**, so the work must be done delicately in a sensitive
environment.

## 2. What the work requires (full requirements list)

| # | Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Access strategy | Preferred: remove the overflow assembly from above if it can be done without damaging hidden plumbing. Fallback (recommended by 5+ licensed plumbers): access from below through the ceiling of the storage unit below the tub. |
| 2 | Plumbing scope | Remove the stuck trip-lever assembly; clean, repair, or replace the waste-and-overflow assembly; restore a working drain. |
| 3 | Drywall scope | Open the ceiling drywall for access if needed; after plumbing is complete, patch/finish the ceiling opening. |
| 4 | One-vendor preference | Prefer a single service that covers **both plumbing and drywall** (all-in-one). |
| 5 | Credentials | Licensed plumber (CA C-36) or licensed contractor; verify license before hiring. |
| 6 | Environment | Occupied unit with tenants — dust, noise, and water disruption must be minimized; work must be delicate and carefully scoped. |
| 7 | Deliverables | Written itemized quote; confirmation of above-access vs below-access approach and the associated drywall patch; schedule coordinated with tenants. |
